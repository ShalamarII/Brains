Shri woke up yawning and stretching, her pillow wet from her drool. The sun just peeked over the horizon, seemingly as tired as Shri herself. Shri struggled to get ready, sloppily tying her hair in a bun, putting on the dress she'd set out last night. The silver-white of the veil glistened in the sunlight, casting beautiful rays of light that made her look ethereal. She gave herself a final look in the mirror and left the room pained. It would be the day she signed away her life. She stepped into the throne room, her father sitting there, a trying look upon his face. He could only find offense with her, no beauty, no kindness. Perhaps she was too much like her late mother, a pristine figure of crystal and glass, brought to life by magic fantastic. She had been expensive prize, costing more lives than any one person is worth. But her father said he could not live without her. From the moment he saw her, she had to be his. So here I am, to watch the friend I adore, the woman I grew up with, to be wed to a man she has hardly met, let alone love. 

## Shri

I hate him. I look away from my father, that grimace he always keeps looking me over in scrutiny before waving his hand like I am to be some acceptable thing. I meet eyes with Pryll, her pain scarcely perceptible, except to my eyes. To me, all color has drained from her eyes and all hope from her mind. I tire of this story, my life a book to be written by anyone but myself. He walks in, handsome as ever, but not the man I love. Sure, he is charming, kind, and has a lovely smile. But he isn't for me. A boom sounds out somewhere to my left and shouting sings like a bell in my ear. Pryll gets up and sprints to me, taking me by the hand and running back to my chambers. My father shouts something at us, but I don't hear it.
