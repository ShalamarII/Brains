My green light is improving. Improving has been important to me for a very long time, and has greatly affected who I am. 
My parents tell me stories of when I was young and I was almost obsessed with learning. Throughout my life I've taken up many a hobby, but none that affected me as largely as video games has.
In my juvenile years I would often spend time with my cousin Eric and we would play video games. I desperately wanted to be the best there was and would think of new things and practice the actions on my own.
I had a fascination with always having more things I could get better at in the game; timing the jumps correctly, hitting an enemy just right, or even skipping portions of the game all together.
I would try to hang with Eric as much as humanly possible, which led to us becoming inseperable, even to this day. We used to play anything we could get our hands on. 
These memories reinforced my love and interest in improving as it was a constant competition between us who could do better or faster.

	In a twist of events, I almost completely lost my desire to learn. This was through 5th grade, in which I was frequently bullied ann ridiculed.
The emotions that I experienced made me want to switch up my entire personality. I went from being a quiet, nerdy guy to a racous, still nerdy, individual.
I was convinced that if I changed, people's perception of me would changed. I tried for about 3 years. 
During this time I experienced multiple breakdowns and depression which I took as far as attempting to take my own life.
I became angry, convinced that if I just became more like my peers I could become better and more liked. All it did was make the people close to me resent me.
My relationship with the majority of my immediate family began to deteriorate, and I became obsessed with improving it.

	This led me into a phase of my life where I began to manipulate people. It didn't matter how close you were to me, I would try to manipulate you.
Not to hurt the person I was manipulating, but to ensure that I could be happy through success. I used to look forward to manipulating others, to the point where I would tell people what their reaction would be or things about them.
People used to get angry with me about this,  but I had long stopped caring about what they thought. Instead I began caring more and more about how I could get exactly what I wanted, when I wanted it.
This mad me become even more secluded, and though I easily made friends, I never could keep them unless we saw each other a bunch.

	My 8th grade year I went to Pennfield Middle School. At PMS I quickly made friends, and my situation began to improve. I could be who I really was around them, and they liked me for it.
I felt like a new person. I started healing, but my underlying anger was still there. I did everything possible to return to the person I was in my youth, but I failed to realize you can't just regress like that.
I am my experiences. I'll never escape who I am and I shouldn't try. So I improved. I read books (one of the few habits I didn't lose) on how to be a gentleman, watched TED talks, and did research on how to become a better person.
But once again, though I was improving I began to "see" girls. In my youth and middle school years I never really thought about girls I was too busy focusing on the bad things. But, ever since I was young I had a fascination with marriage.
The thought of being able to tackle the world with someone else just captivated me. I wasn't commonly in relationships so I looked to people that were constantly in relationships on how to attract others. 
Keep in mind that this was 8-9th grade so I just became even more immature, prioritizing laughter over my actual personality and who I was. This led me to fall back into old habits. 
I've never been good at breaking habits, and I was weaning myself then anyways so I fell, hard. I talked differently, switched up my morals and tried to improve my attractiveness to girls.
But I never would date. Not because people didn't like me; because of my obsession with marriage. I never deemed myself good enough for marriage and stopped myself before I could get in any relationships.
My improvement felt as if it was naught for anything. I was so willing to change who I actually was that I didn't realize I didn't know who I was.
Like Gatsby I was searching for the past. Obsessed with the fact that my life was "Ideal" or "Better" back then.

	This lead me to search and find it. What was the thing that was consistent through my entire life and all things, affecting my career to even the smallest details of my life.
I searched long and hard and found it is my love to learn. My proclivity to be "better", whether it really was or not in my mind. I did research on it, talked to my parents and friends about it and the answer was always yes.
Improvement has been pushing me for so long, and not necessarily in good ways. In each time it was based on what I thought in the moment. If I could go back and "improve", a very small amount of my choices would be the same.
But I have to live with that. That's who I was, am, and will be. An improver, a dreamer, and a bumbling fool all in one. Improvement is my green light, and it continues to affect me even when I am writing this.
