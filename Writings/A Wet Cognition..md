Feeling is odd, a hole in the sock of my mind where my impressions leak, wetting the cognition. Even since childhood we must usurp, refine, and control our emotions lest we ruin ourselves. Logic is naturally governed by emotion, and it pesters me. I must watch myself in any moment, that my biases and emotions are not robbing me of my truer faculties. It is tiring, running so much through that "filter" of mine, but to me it is imperative. It is the minimum to cultivate myself. It is somewhat alienating to know that many people can go without questioning their beliefs; or when they do, question those value so loosely as to perch these beliefs higher. I have never found myself in this contempt of the mind. I often think of myself from the lens of a book. I see myself in different shades, Primrose, Xanadu, Saffron. Portions of myself that interact with the world - that interact within myself. I do not think it is normal, but it is what I have become. There is no quintessential I. There is an amalgam of code-switching, vocal management, and careful weight. This pressure overtakes me, lights my cognition on fire. I must be remarkable now, each step purposeful and controlled, each word measured and polite. I may not stumble, and especially not fall, lest I be the fool I am played by me. To die is to give up, to cry is to give in, to be weak is to give out; and what a shame that is, to be known. I am a tool, my life meant to provide some end. You cannot simply be, that would be against everything you were; everything everyone was. Don't know your ancestors worked for this? How can you squander this opportunity, this gift. How dare you? You'd better not screw this up. Generational curses will be broken with you. You are smart, talented, thoughtful. You are measured, precise, obedient. Take this paint I gave you and make my magnum opus. For you are creating our history. But if I snap, if I become stretched beyond my limits and break, a stained glass image of perfection reduced to dust; what am I? Was I always meant to go somewhere, to be remarkable? Am I still that boy you saw hope in, showing you his books? Or am I that boy who knew too much; that was right too often, that acted far too grown. Am I remarkable? Am I destined to break these chains? Or am I one of them, the family talked about in hushed tones, invisible daggers stabbing their back. Am I tainted by the world, a pristine figure taken by lesser vices. Or might I be more?
